TITLE: Aeronautical information
SUBJECT: 010 08 Aeronautical information
SUMMARY: Annex 15: the AIP and its parts, amendments and supplements, NOTAM and ASHTAM, AIC, the AIRAC cycle, and pre-flight information.
---
[chime]
K: Welcome back to Cleared for Theory, Air Law. Topic eight is aeronautical information. It's a short topic with predictable questions.
T: What's the main publication?
K: The AIP, the aeronautical information publication. It has three parts: GEN, which is general, E-N-R, which is en-route, and A-D, which is aerodromes. GEN also lists each State's differences from ICAO standards.
T: How does the AIP get changed?
K: Permanent changes come as AIP Amendments. Temporary changes lasting three months or more, or short-term information with a lot of text or graphics, come as AIP Supplements.
T: And NOTAMs?
K: A NOTAM is sent by telecommunication. It announces that a facility, service, procedure or hazard has been set up or changed, when pilots need to know quickly. An ASHTAM is a special NOTAM about volcanic ash.
T: What about the circulars?
K: An aeronautical information circular, or A-I-C, carries information that fits neither the AIP nor a NOTAM. That means long-term forecasts of major changes in rules, explanations, advice, and administrative matters.
T: And AIRAC?
K: AIRAC bundles significant changes onto common effective dates every twenty-eight days. The information is sent out at least forty-two days ahead, so it reaches users at least twenty-eight days before. For major changes, fifty-six days ahead is recommended.
T: Twenty-eight, forty-two, fifty-six.
K: Two more facts. If an information service isn't open twenty-four hours, it must be available while aircraft are flying in its area, plus at least two hours before and after. And before a flight you get a pre-flight information bulletin, which summarises the current NOTAMs. After the flight, report any problems with facilities, and any birds you saw, back to the information service.
